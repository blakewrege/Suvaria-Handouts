The Pink Pony Inn¶

  • Type: Bardcore tavern, cabaret, and suspiciously social inn
  • Location: Armskirk's entertainment district
  • Owner: Madam Maeve Mane
  • Head bartender: Butch Glassidy
  • Famous regular: Clint Wetwood, owner of Wetwood's Brokeback Outfitters
  • Known for: Live music, shifter performers, private booths, theatrical drinks, and professionally maintained plausible deniability
  • House motto: “Where the fur flies and the drinks bite back.”

The Pink Pony Inn is what happens when a spaghetti western rides into a gay furry cabaret and neither one agrees to leave. Rose-colored lanterns shine over a weathered wooden bar, swinging doors open with unnecessary drama, and every musical set appears to end in a high-noon stare-down between a bard and someone wearing magnificent heels.

The house band plays lute-heavy remixes of songs that sound suspiciously modern. Feline ribbon dancers, lupine stompers, avian aerialists, visiting musicians, and enthusiastic amateurs rotate through the stage. The audience is encouraged to sing along, tip generously, and refrain from asking why the bartender already knows their name.

The Pink Pony welcomes travelers, performers, queer locals, shifters, adventurers, political staffers pretending not to be political staffers, and anyone willing to behave themselves badly. Its curtained booths are famous for private conversations. Privacy is not guaranteed, but the lighting is extremely forgiving.

Clint Wetwood, the Otter with No Shame¶

Clint Wetwood is an openly gay otter-shifter high-country guide, ram wrangler, river-crossing specialist, and full-time argument against subtlety. He owns Wetwood's Brokeback Outfitters, but spends enough evenings at the Pink Pony to qualify as furniture—very attractive, unusually damp furniture. He has an athletic swimmer's build, deliberately damp brown hair, expressive whiskers, fitted sleeveless leathers, a cropped blue-green sash, and enough jewelry to make every entrance look choreographed.

Clint grew up among Armskirk's canal workers, ferrymen, river guides, and dockside couriers. He swam messages through flooded tunnels, retrieved cargo from dangerous currents, and won illegal canal races by choosing routes usually described as “technically water.” He eventually took those skills uphill and opened an outfitting business specializing in discreet mountain excursions for bachelors, confirmed bachelors, and men who become confirmed somewhere above the tree line.

Excursions include sturdy horses, matching chaps, private cabins, ram-watching at sunset, and the famous One Bedroll Discount, which no customer has ever claimed accidentally. Brokeback also handles bookings, equipment, and transport for Putty Nutty Caverns, its family-friendly underground attraction. Clint insists that what happens above the tree line stays above the tree line—unless the bard writes a song—and that everything below it has been thoroughly reviewed by counsel.

He dances when the music is good, heckles when it is bad, and flirts with the confidence of a man who has never mistaken embarrassment for a useful emotion. Clint is not technically one of the dancers. This distinction becomes less defensible as the evening progresses.

“Darling, everyone in here is lying. The trick is knowing who is lying professionally.”

“If you want secrets, buy a drink. If you want truth, buy three. If you want my number, ask like a gentleman.”

“Bareback is a personal decision. The insurance company requires saddles.”

Butch Glassidy, Head Bartender¶

Butch Glassidy is a broad bison shifter with polished horns, rolled sleeves, and the fastest pour in Armskirk's west end. His name produces expectations that his quiet husband, delicate embroidery hobby, and prize-winning miniature rose garden consistently refuse to satisfy.

Butch runs the bar with deadpan efficiency while Clint runs his mouth nearby. He rarely flirts, never hurries, and can end an argument by placing the correct drink in front of each participant before either remembers ordering.

“You can have it neat, dirty, or with plausible deniability.”

Madam Maeve Mane¶

The Pink Pony belongs to Madam Maeve Mane, an impeccably dressed mare shifter and retired stage hostess who moves through the room with the unsettling calm of someone who has seen tonight's trouble rehearse before. Maeve rarely tends bar, never raises her voice, and can stop a fight by asking one participant whether this is truly the story they meant to tell about themselves.

She leaves the bar to Butch and the nightly spectacle to whoever survives Clint, but controls the bookings, payroll, booth assignments, and quiet removal of patrons who confuse hospitality with ownership. Maeve's rule is simple: every guest may choose who they are inside the Pink Pony, provided they respect everyone else's choice too.

House Attractions¶

  • A Fistful of Coppers is the early show: affordable drinks, loud music, and performers building toward decisions they will defend as artistic.
  • For a Few Coppers More is the late show, when the costumes improve and judgment becomes a limited resource.
  • The Magnificent Seven-Inch Heels is a recurring revue whose title is both accurate and structurally ambitious.
  • The Good, the Bad, and the Thirsty is Butch's three-drink tasting flight. Customers are invited to determine which glass is which before the glasses begin offering opinions.

Popular drinks include the Tail Twister, Foxfire Fizz, Moonhowl Mead, Glow Cloud Cooler, and glittering Pink Pony House Shot. The Glow Cloud Cooler is swirling, citrus-flavored, and faintly aware. If the Glow Cloud appears, finish your drink.

House Rules¶

  • No biting without consent.
  • No shedding on the stage.
  • Tip the performers.
  • The Dog Park is not discussed here.
  • Draw attention freely; draw steel reluctantly.
  • If Clint says, “Duck,” he may mean the verb, the animal, or someone you dated. React first and clarify later.

Familiar Faces¶

Deep Snoat, Armskirk's discreet broker of rumors and leverage, is associated with one of the Pink Pony's curtained booths. Calling Snoat a gossip is like calling a crossbow a pointed suggestion.
