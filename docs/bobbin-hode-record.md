# 🪶 Backstory
Bobbin Hode’s earliest years are hard to pin down, and he usually avoids questions about where he was born with a grin, a shrug, or a convenient change of subject. What he does speak of, when he is being honest, is his mother.
She was from **Leonia**, and when Bobbin was very young, she brought him to **Armskirk**. They lived there for a few short years, in a cramped neighborhood full of rain-slick streets, shouting vendors, laundry lines, and music spilling from tavern doors. Bobbin remembers her warmth, her stubbornness, and the way she always seemed to know when trouble was coming.
His father is a blank spot in the story. He may still be alive, or he may simply have been absent for so long that it no longer mattered. Bobbin either does not know the truth or has chosen not to chase it.
When Bobbin was about seven years old, his mother died. After that, Armskirk became too large, too cold, and too dangerous for a child on his own. He survived by stealing when he had to, running when he could, and learning very quickly which people could be trusted and which ones smiled only to bite.
Eventually, Bobbin left Leonia behind. However it happened, he wound up in the **Tall Garden Dominion**, where the forests were broad, the roads were dangerous, and the gap between nobles and common folk was wide enough to hide a rebellion in.

# 🌲 100 Acre Thieves Forest
The **100 Acre Thieves Forest** is a thick temperate deciduous forest laced with rivers flowing down from the **Murky Mountains**. A main path winds through the woods toward the city of **Duloc**, but many who stay on that path too long find that someone is watching them from the trees.
The forest is notorious for its burrowing **ankheg**, which make travel dangerous for anyone unfamiliar with the land. According to local legend, only **Henry** truly knows how to avoid them, though whether this is wisdom, luck, or drunken confidence depends on who is telling the story.
Hidden deep within the forest lie the ruins of **Narag-Dum**, an old dwarven fortress built atop the remains of a far older **Yuan-ti** city. Most folk know only that the ruins exist. Smugglers, treasure-seekers, and fools tend to learn more than they wanted.
For Bobbin, the 100 Acre Thieves Forest became more than a hideout. It became a home. Its paths, rivers, and hidden clearings shaped him into the outlaw the common people would come to admire and the nobles would come to despise.

# 🌿 Tall Garden and Ron Stayark
In Tall Garden, Bobbin found a cause as much as a country. The forests hid desperate villagers, poachers, smugglers, rebels, and tax collectors—sometimes all on the same road. Bobbin quickly learned that stealing from the powerful felt much better when the spoils went to people who were hungry, hunted, or hopeless.
It was there that he met **Ron Stayark**, long before Ron became king. The most common version of the story says Bobbin tried to rob Ron, got caught, and then talked his way into a friendship instead of a hanging. Whatever the truth, the two became close allies. Ron had a conscience too large for court politics, and Bobbin had no respect at all for unjust authority, so they suited each other surprisingly well.
As King Boffery Jaratheon’s rule grew more vain, cruel, and absurd, Bobbin became one of the loudest thorns in the regime’s side. He took on the alias **Robyn Hode**, gathered a band of outlaws around him, and turned mischief into resistance.

# 🏹 The Crooked Company
Bobbin became the leader of the **Crooked Company**, a merry band of thieves, misfits, drunkards, performers, and rebels who robbed the corrupt, fed the poor, and humiliated the powerful whenever possible. They harassed tax caravans, embarrassed petty officials, and left behind enough songs and stories to make it difficult to tell where history ended and legend began.
The Crooked Company became famous enough that the **Sheriff of Duloc** placed a bounty of **100 gold pieces** on Bobbin Hode, dead or alive. Bobbin, naturally, considered this flattering.
When Boffery’s regime cracked down, the Crooked Company scattered. Some vanished into the woods. Some went to ground in villages and monasteries. Some kept the old songs alive in taverns. Bobbin helped **Ron Stayark** escape to safety and kept moving, living long enough to become both a hero and a wanted nuisance.


# 💖 Current Arc
Now traveling alongside the magical chaos that is **Amerilis Liadon**, Bobbin is trying very hard to become “noble,” or at least noble-adjacent.
He calls her “milady,” overcooks flambéed meals in her honor, writes terrible poetry, and trains daily as though enough effort might turn him into the sort of man she already deserves. Beneath the showmanship, however, he is sincere: fiercely loyal, surprisingly thoughtful, and willing to throw himself into danger without hesitation.
Now that he finds himself back in **Leonia**, Bobbin also feels the pull of something more personal. He wants to revisit the place in **Armskirk** where his mother once lived. He has not said much about it yet, but the desire has been sitting in him for years.

That return leads through **Lovers’ Lookout** and **the house below it**. The romantic outing begins warmly before Bobbin recognizes Candlewick Row.
