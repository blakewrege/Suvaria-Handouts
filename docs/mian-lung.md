# Mian Lung

![Mian Lung beneath the Golden Dragon](https://i.imgur.com/SD5Lgky.png)

- **Type:** Province of the [Republic of Leonia](republic-of-leonia.md)
- **Historic name:** The Jade Kingdom
- **Location:** Eastern coast of Leonia
- **People:** The Mian
- **Provincial leader:** Chancellor Jack E. Chan
- **Known for:** Soldiers, sword saints, river trade, old traditions

Mian Lung is an old eastern realm inside a young republic. Its history predates Leonia, its people retain a strong identity of their own, and its soldiers form the backbone of the republic's armed forces.

The name **Mian Lung** is commonly translated as “Distant from the Dragon.” Mian tradition says its people are ancient wanderers from a faraway kingdom called **Shou Lung**, “Receiver of the Dragon.” The journey is too old to verify and too good a story to stop telling.

## Land and People

Mian Lung stretches along Leonia's eastern coastline. River valleys, wooded foothills, fishing villages, fortified settlements, and old city-states shape the province. **Xuán Ji**, the “Mysterious River,” is among its best-known regions. For generations, the noble Fang family supervised trade along its river and foothills.

The Mian have a reputation for discipline, duty, skilled craftsmanship, and formidable martial traditions. Fishing, trapping, river trade, farming, forestry, and military service have all shaped life here. Urban expansion has also consumed forests and other natural resources, driving old nature spirits deeper into the wilds. Mian Lung has excellent roads to history and several potholes to modernity.

### Deuce Leem, Guardian of Xuán Ji

**Deuce Leem** is a powerful water spirit traditionally said to guard the Xuán Ji. He seldom leaves the river, preferring to answer problems by flowing around them, through them, or—when the problem is an invading army—directly over them.

During the War of Broken Chains, trapped Mian soldiers beat the old water drums and called for aid at Eclipse Ford. Deuce answered from Xuán Ji and strengthened the failing sluice flood that broke the New Moon Battalion. Lincohn handled the vampire officers; Deuce handled the water; generations of playwrights have handled the credit.

Mian martial artists often repeat his best-known advice: **“Be water, my friend.”** Shrines depict him in dozens of contradictory fighting stances, but all agree that his hair looked excellent in the rain.

## Current Crisis: The Fires in Mian Lung

![The fires spreading across Mian Lung](https://i.imgur.com/RqNSp9J.png)

Mian Lung is burning again. A new order called the **Jǐn Bào**, or “Golden Leopards,” claims it will restore balance through fire. Its leader, **Master Zhaoyan**, preaches purity and obedience to the flame.

The results are less poetic than the speeches. Villages have vanished, temples have fallen silent, and the **Mógu Chóng Bài** have gone into hiding. Refugees describe rivers black with ash and feline shadows moving across rooftops at dawn.

Fang Ro received a scroll sealed in red wax and bearing her name in Infernal. She recognizes the Jǐn Bào's rhetoric from earlier violence and believed she had already ended whatever movement inspired it. Fang has left the party and returned to Mian Lung to aid her homeland; Black Chao has joined the party in her absence.

What the Jǐn Bào truly wants, whether the reports of feline shadows are literal, and who sent Fang's infernal message remain unknown. Mian Lung currently has more questions than answers and significantly more fire than either.

## From Jade Kingdom to Leonian Province

About two hundred years ago, an earthquake in the Qlohsh Highlands unleashed an infernal army. A breakaway force marched east, destroyed settlements, and threatened Mian Lung before warnings could reach the province.

Mian Lung fought that incursion as the independent Jade Kingdom. During the same broad era of regional danger, Armskirk, Torath, and Corvos formed their own mutual-protection pact. Their alliance became the Republic of Leonia; Mian Lung was not one of its founding members.

After Leonia existed, the Jade Kingdom signed a separate mutual-defense treaty with the new republic. The two states cooperated against common threats, but Mian Lung remained sovereign. About one hundred years ago, the **Jade-Lion Concord** made Mian Lung a full province of Leonia. The agreement preserved the office of Chancellor and many local institutions while formalizing military service, taxes, food levies, and Mian representation in national government.

Leonian historians call this “full inclusion.” Mian historians often call it “the second agreement, with considerably more fine print.”

## Government and Leonia

Mian Lung is governed locally by **Chancellor Jack E. Chan**, who also represents the province before the Ringleader's government. The province retains older laws, customs, titles, and institutions so long as they do not conflict with the Leonian charter.

Mian Lung pays substantial tax and food levies to the republic and supplies a large share of its professional soldiers. The Mian have honored those obligations for generations, though “honored” and “enjoyed” are very different verbs.

### The Nine Tables and One Ballot

Mian Lung elects its Chancellor every six years through the **Nine Tables and One Ballot**. Prospective candidates must first earn endorsements from nine traditional constituencies: farmers, fishers, soldiers, artisans, merchants, scholars, temples, city wards, and rural clans. They then face a day-long public examination conducted at nine literal tables. The tests include interpreting law, balancing a granary ledger, settling a mock boundary dispute, planning a flood evacuation, and brewing tea while elderly spectators loudly explain how the previous candidate did it better.

Anyone who survives all nine tables without fleeing, bribing an examiner, or declaring war on the audience earns a place on the ballot. The province then chooses its Chancellor by ranked-choice vote. This preserves the ancient principle that leaders should possess wisdom, patience, practical skill, and at least one afternoon they will never discuss again.

The victor is inaugurated beneath an enormous golden dragon puppet carried by eighty volunteers. Tradition requires the dragon to bow before the new Chancellor. Tradition also forbids enlarging the palace doors, so the inauguration has taken place outside for 312 consecutive years.

The Chancellor administers Mian Lung's provincial budget, granaries, roads, canals, civil defense, local courts, and civil appointments. The office may mobilize provincial forces during an immediate emergency, but sustained national deployment belongs to Leonia's Master of the Muster.

At the Hall of Accord, the Chancellor occupies the separate **Jade Seat** rather than joining the six-member Barker Circle. The Jade Seat has a binding vote on changes to the Jade-Lion Concord, Mian taxes and food levies, deployment of Mian forces beyond the province, and national laws written specifically for Mian Lung. On other national business, the Chancellor may speak, advise, object, and produce an intimidating quantity of documentation, but does not cast a Barker vote.

### Chancellor Jack E. Chan

<img src="https://i.imgur.com/pqlwZin.png" alt="Chancellor Jack E. Chan" width="180">

Jack E. Chan is a former civil-defense organizer and treaty negotiator who would rather fix a problem himself than order someone else into danger. He is warm, practical, self-deprecating, and remarkably capable, though rarely dignified while demonstrating it.

He has been known to catch falling scrolls, teacups, and pieces of furniture without interrupting a meeting. When violence is unavoidable, he favors improvised objects, rapid movement, and apologizing to whatever he breaks. He has an ancient and deeply personal feud with ladders.

Chan supports the republic but argues that Leonia too often treats Mian Lung as a barracks and pantry rather than a full province. He wants fairer levies, greater provincial investment, and recognition for what the Mian contribute beyond military service.

His greatest weakness is that he delegates poorly. Chan will exhaust himself protecting others long after a wiser leader would have asked for help.

> “Government is like a ladder: useful, necessary, and always falling toward the person least prepared to catch it.”

## The Martial State

Military service is central to Mian Lung's public identity. In ordinary times, its forces range from local levies and trained infantry to cavalry, **shi** knights, and elite companies. During major incursions, conscription may be imposed.

The most celebrated elite warriors are the **jiàn shèng**, or “sword saints.” They train from youth with the jiàn and horn bow, combining discipline, mobility, and an alarming ability to turn “unarmored” into a threat.

The **Night Tigers** are an elite Mian company commanded by **Heise Chao Ping**, better known as **Black Chao**. The company earned its reputation surviving infernal attacks and later developed difficult war games designed to remove the undeserving, the soft, and anyone who thought training was going unusually well.

## The Fang and the Bái Láng

The Fang of Xuán Ji were ennobled before Leonia existed. Their patriarch traditionally bore the title **Gong**, or Duke. During the first great infernal incursion, the Fang lands were overwhelmed until the neighboring **Bái Láng**, or “White Wolves,” came to their aid.

The Bái Láng are **yaoguài**: natural shapeshifters with human, animal, and hybrid forms. They are not lycanthropes, do not spread their nature through bites, and are no more beholden to the moon than an ordinary wolf. Fang and Bái Láng families later intermarried, producing bloodlines touched by shapeshifting magic.

That union was once considered holy. As Mian society became more urban and distant from the old spirits, mixed-blood Fang fell in social standing and were often treated as wild, unreliable, or “impure.” Many moved away from major settlements and worked as hunters, trappers, fishers, and farmers. The prejudice remains part of Mian Lung's history—and, in some places, its present.

## Faith and the Deep Places

Beneath the foothills of Xuán Ji lie caves and hidden temples associated with the **Mógu Chóng Bài**, the “Cult of the Fungus.” Its spore druids protect underground places and preserve traditions overlooked by the growing city-states.

The Mógu are neither a single cheerful mushroom club nor a single sinister cult. Like most religious communities, they include teachers, guardians, mystics, zealots, and at least one person who has made the meeting unnecessarily complicated.

## Fang Ro and Black Chao

**Fang Ro**, also called **Seiqua**, was born in Xuán Ji to Tengfei and Lian. Descended from both Fang and Bái Láng bloodlines, she learned wilderness skills from her fisherman and trapper father, trained with the Mógu Chóng Bài, and later became a jiàn shèng in the Mian army.

An infernal attack led by Ulysses Vex destroyed her village and killed her family. Fang Ro survived, but Vex severed her tongue with the hell-forged blade Gloomfire. Her pursuit of Vex eventually carried her away from Mian Lung and into the party's adventures.

**Black Chao** is a Mian infantry leader and commander of the Night Tigers. He lost his left eye during the same campaign and survived to rebuild the company around its remaining veterans. Chao considers Fang Ro a little sister and sometimes calls her **Heise Ro**, or “Black Ro,” as a sign of kinship—even when disagreeing loudly with nearly every decision she makes.

General **Wei Shen**, currently Leonia's Master of the Muster, is also Mian-born and remains deeply respected among the province's soldiers and veterans.

## Mian Lung Beyond the Province

Generations of Mian merchants, sailors, artisans, soldiers, and laborers have settled elsewhere in Leonia. Their most visible community is **Xiaolong**, the “Little Dragon” district of [Armskirk](armskirk.md).

Xiaolong's lantern festivals, tea houses, martial schools, bathhouses, herbalists, shrines, import halls, and food stalls make it a cultural bridge between Mian Lung and the Free Cities. Residents often consider themselves fully Leonian and proudly Mian, demonstrating that identity can do two things at once even when government paperwork cannot.
